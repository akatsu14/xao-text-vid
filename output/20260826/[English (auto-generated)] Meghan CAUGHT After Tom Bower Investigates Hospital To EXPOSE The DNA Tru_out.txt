For years, a storm of doubt has gathered around the births of the Duke and Duchess of Sussex’s two children. THE GHOSTS OF PORTLAND HOSPITAL

A royal birth is not a private family affair; it is a meticulously documented constitutional event. For centuries, the arrival of a potential heir to the throne has been subject to rigorous protocol, a bulwark against the historical threats of changelings and succession plots that once plunged the kingdom into bloody civil war. While the archaic tradition of having the Home Secretary present in the delivery room was abolished by King George VI, the underlying principle of unimpeachable verification remains. A royal birth leaves an indelible footprint: a phalanx of named physicians, signed declarations, security logs, nursing staff rosters, and official palace announcements with precise timings. It is a fortress of paperwork designed to be impenetrable.

It was this fortress that Tom Bower and his team of researchers set out to inspect. Their first target was The Portland Hospital in London, the prestigious private institution where Archie Harrison Mountbatten-Windsor was officially declared to have been born on May 6th, 2019. The team searched for the standard, non-negotiable evidence. They looked for the official confirmations, the staff who could speak on the record, the kind of institutional paper trail that a high-profile birth of this magnitude would inevitably generate. According to sources close to the investigation, they found a void. There was no verifiable royal paper trail, no chorus of medical professionals confirming their attendance. The event, in an official documentary sense, was a ghost.

The search then moved across the Atlantic, to the Santa Barbara Cottage Hospital in California, the stated birthplace of Lilibet Diana Mountbatten-Windsor in June 2021. The result was disturbingly familiar. Again, a vacuum of verifiable, on-the-record proof. In the place of certainty, there was only a carefully worded statement from the Sussexes’ press team. Royal historians and constitutional experts, speaking on background, express a deep sense of unease. “For a birth within the top ten of the line of succession to be so opaque is more than just a break with tradition,” one prominent historian noted. “It is a serious institutional vulnerability. The entire purpose of the monarchy’s structure is to provide absolute, unshakeable certainty on succession. Any ambiguity, any informational black hole, is corrosive to the Crown itself.”

The silence from the medical community is, for many, the most damning piece of circumstantial evidence. Not a single doctor or nurse has ever come forward to attach their name and professional reputation to the births. In a world of tell-all books and lucrative media deals, the complete absence of any firsthand medical account is statistically baffling. This has led critics to a stark conclusion: that no one is speaking because there is nothing to say. Meghan’s legal team, known for its aggressive, hair-trigger litigation against media outlets, has remained silent in the face of Bower’s published claims. He put the allegation of missing records in his book, *Revenge*, a direct challenge inviting a lawsuit. None came. The reason, legal analysts suggest, is simple: a defamation suit would trigger the legal process of discovery, compelling the Duchess to produce under oath the very documents Tom Bower claims do not exist.

A CONSPIRACY WRITTEN IN INK

If the absence of evidence at the hospitals was alarming, the evidence that *did* emerge from the UK’s own government offices was, for investigators, a bombshell. The focus shifted to a single, critical document: the certified copy of Archie’s official UK birth record, issued by the General Register Office. The world was told, and the official announcements confirmed, that Archie was born on May 6th, 2019. Yet, a document circulated by critics, purported to be a certified copy, bears a chilling detail. At the bottom of the certificate, the date of registration is listed as May 1st, 2019—five full days *before* the child was allegedly born.

In the United Kingdom, as in virtually every modern legal system, the pre-registration of a human birth is a physical and legal impossibility. A person cannot be entered into the state’s records before they exist. The law is absolute, with no exceptions for royalty or rank. The document, if authentic, is not just an anomaly; it is evidence of a profound and illegal irregularity at the heart of the state’s own record-keeping. When inquiries were reportedly made to the registrar’s office, the response was a masterpiece of bureaucratic deflection, with an official stating they could only confirm that “the register reports the details as they are presented to them on real time.” This did nothing to quell the firestorm, instead suggesting the system had recorded information it had been given, however impossible it seemed.

The irregularities continued to stack up. The process of registering Archie’s birth was itself a bizarre departure from all norms. Instead of the parents attending the registrar’s office, the deputy registrar was summoned to Frogmore Cottage, a highly unusual concession. More disturbing, however, was the evidence presented. Every new parent in the UK is issued a “Red Book” by the hospital or midwife at the moment of birth. This Personal Child Health Record contains the baby’s weight, time of birth, and other vital statistics, signed by the attending medical professional. It is the foundational medical passport of a British citizen and is the standard proof required for birth registration.

According to inside reports, no Red Book was ever shown to the registrar. Instead, she was handed a handwritten note, a piece of paper provided not by a doctor, but by Prince Harry’s personal protection officer. This note, astonishingly, was said to have included a legal disclaimer, a handwritten sentence protecting the bodyguard from any liability should the information he was presenting prove to be false. A source familiar with UK registration law described the situation as “beyond comprehension.” “The state’s official registrar accepted, as proof of a new heir to the throne, an unverified, handwritten note from a bodyguard who himself was indemnified against the information being fraudulent. It reads like a plot from a political thriller, not a procedure of the British state.” This chain of events transformed the narrative from one of private choices and broken traditions to one of potential, literal fraud against the government.

In any normal court of law, this would end the matter. But the British monarchy is not governed by normal laws. It is governed by a thousand years of constitutional precedent, at the heart of which is the principle of corporeal birth.

The law of succession, derived from the Act of Settlement and centuries of common law, is brutally specific. It does not merely require a genetic link. It demands that an heir must be “born of thebody” of the titled royal wife. The child must be physically carried and delivered by the woman married to the royal in question. This law was designed in a pre-scientific age to eliminate any possibility of fraud, to ensure that a surrogate baby or a swapped infant could never be smuggled onto the throne. If Meghan Markle used a surrogate—a claim her own half-sister, Samantha Markle, has publicly made—then a DNA test is worse than useless. It would prove her genetic maternity while simultaneously opening the door to a legal investigation into the physical birth itself. The Crown would be forced to subpoena the hospital records, and if those records show a surrogate delivery or, as Bower alleges, simply do not exist, the children are instantly and automatically disqualified from the line of succession.

The very act of faking a pregnancy, of parading in public with what critics have viciously labeled a “moon bump” while a surrogate carried the child, would then be re-contextualized. It would cease to be a matter of privacy and become a deliberate, calculated plot to deceive the Crown and the British people, placing an ineligible child in the line of succession. This is where the whispers of the most severe crime in the British legal canon begin: treason. The Treason Act of 1351 is an ancient law, but it remains on the statute books. One of its clauses pertains to the violation of the line of succession. While modern prosecutors would face an unprecedented challenge in applying it to surrogacy, constitutional hardliners argue that a conspiracy to fraudulently place an illegitimate heir in line to the throne is the very definition of an attack on the sovereign. The Sussexes would not just lose their titles; they could, in the most extreme interpretation, face criminal charges for perpetrating a fraud against the constitution itself.
