On the twenty-eighth of March in the year two thousand and twenty-six there was a Filipino singer of twenty-seven years of age who was standing under the brilliant lights of the London Palladium and preparing to change the course of his life from that moment on. He was a blank canvas for the millions watching on the comforts of their own home. Simon Cowell, Amanda Holden, Alesha Dixon and Bruno Tonioli saw only one person – a scared and optimistic hopeful with a karaoke standard and a well-rehearsed tale about a grandma who was helping out. They could not have known that they were looking at a man who had previously tasted the thrill of the ascension and the tragedy of the fall of a music career in the Philippines, who had buried one professional identity to invent another. They had no means of knowing this. They didn’t see the waitress who had spent two years ferrying dishes between singing sessions in Glasgow. Nor did they see the strategist who had secretly picked the one song in the world he thought could shatter Simon Cowell’s legendary defenses. Neither of these people was visible to them. Instead they saw a singer. Before the first note left Matty’s mouth, he gave the judges the actual deal on his condition. In his speech he spoke about the years he would always be the loser in every competition he entered. In his comments he sounded the resignation you'd expect from a man who's come to terms with his own apparent shortcomings. He made his resignation in a matter of sentences. And though it was a preamble they had heard a thousand times before, the judges looked sweetly and sympathetically to the individual speaking. Right after that the theater was filled with the instantly identifiable opening notes of "Purple Rain," and the mood began to change. The performance they had just witnessed was the end result of a strategy that had been in the works for almost a decade, but almost no one knew this fact even though they had just experienced it. Long before the confetti began to rain in London to mark the Olympics, a young child named Matty Juniosa saw that movement was the foundation of his identity. Throughout his life he experienced a tapestry of varied environments. Where was he born and raised? He was born and raised in the busy metro of Quezon City, Philippines. Even before he started high school, his family was already a nomadic one, travelling to places such as Mandaluyong, Zamboanga, Tawi Tawi, Cavite and Taguig. They moved around quite a bit before he began high school. The constant uprooting brought about many changes in his life, particularly when he was in Tawi Tawi, at the very southern tip of the archipelago. It was a faraway place from the cultural core of Manila, a place where a Catholic kid going to get an education in musical theater in Scotland could absorb a sense of home that was flexible and diversified all at the same time. His formative years were spent steeped in the rich cultural fabric of his own nation, which created in him the ability to adapt to new conditions, which would prove to be his most significant attribute. He moved to new places, passed through different halls at school, yet his voice stayed the same throughout his life. This was one item that lasted all his life. By the time he was five years old he was already singing and performing for anyone who would listen.  He’d started doing this already. By the time he enrolled in the secondary school program at Saint Paul College of Makati, he had already turned music from an interest into the defining characteristic of his identity. This metamorphosis happened while he was in the program. He also immersed himself into the merciless world of several local singing competitions, and became part of the highly respected school chorus, Musica Paulinos. He pursued his study at De La Salle University of the Philippines after graduating from high school. It was his debut performance for such a discerning crowd. 2018 was the year of his graduation and he faced the same dilemma every young adult has to face: the option between a secure and predictable road and the terrible unpredictability of chasing a dream. He had to choose between the two. Matty decided he would go after the dream. He made his attempts once he had decided to pursue music with an effort that was unrelenting and all-consuming. Within a year he had reached the audition stage of the first ever season of the Philippine edition of the singing competition Idol. But there he was, suddenly, in front of the judges, his heart racing in his chest, and he was not just a young adult who liked to sing. He had become something far more meaningful. What he didn’t know was that this same ideal was about to be shattered in the most humiliating, public way possible.

The Philippine version of the singing competition Idol had a first season in 2019 and it was a media event that was shown all across the country. The panel of judges for the competition was composed of some of the most well-known musicians in the country, such as the famous Regine Velasquez and the well-liked balladeer Moira Dela Torre. The initial audition resulted in just sixty-nine vocalists earning a golden ticket that allowed them to move forward to the next stage of the competition. This was out of a pool of thousands of applications from throughout the country. The gang included Matty, who was only twenty years old at the time and had just graduated from college. They belonged to the group. He walked into the competition with the unshakeable notion that this was his moment, the culmination of everything he had been working for as he worked towards this moment. He had waited a long time for this moment. His persistence and determination helped him to clear the tough preliminary rounds and get a coveted place in the top twelve live concerts in the competition. The competition challenged the finalists to sing a song written by the renowned Filipino composer Ryan Cayabyab, who personally guided each of the contestants. The first live transmission would be the time this assignment was meant to be completed. The show’s points system, a delicate check and balance, honored the votes of the public no less than the points handed out by the judges. It was evident from this that genius was not enough, and popularity was the most important factor. Matty, on the other hand, was not yet a well known name, despite being a good vocalist. He didn’t have a large fan base, or a viral audition footage to drum up support. The product of the first elimination was made public and the data that were revealed alarmed the general public. Matty was the lowest-scoring finalist among the twelve finalists at the end of the competition. He had an overall score of 50.5 percent. That night he and another participant were cut from the competition. He was the first to fall. While Zephanie Dimaranan was packing his belongings, she was helpless to do anything except watch him. He spoke in a highly professional way but the expression on his face said a whole other thing. His face looked like that of a young man who felt like his one and only shot to pursue a career in music had just been wasted in front of his entire country on live television. He would never have had the chance to pursue a career in music again.

By a strange quirk of fate the network that announced his elimination decided to give him a chance to save his life. It was an odd turn of events. Soon the producers not only welcomed him back, but paired him with two other excellent finalists from his season. They achieved this reputation with their powerful harmonies and exciting shows. They were doing some actual job, true to life, at this time. During the year 2022, the iDolls made a public announcement that they were going to officially end their relationship. Despite the fact that the response was crystal apparent, a significant number of their followers were unable to comprehend it. Matty had been awarded a scholarship that would enable him to attend the Royal Conservatoire of Scotland, which is widely regarded as one of the most prominent educational institutions in Scotland. On the other hand, Matty was of the opinion that this was the only next move that made any sense to take after that.
