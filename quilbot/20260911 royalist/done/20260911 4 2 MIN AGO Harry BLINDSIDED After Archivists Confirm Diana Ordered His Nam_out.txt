THE WEIGHT OF A HIDDEN HISTORY

Here are the vaults containing Queen Victoria's frantic, handwritten letters after the death of Prince Albert, Edward VIII's abdication documents that nearly shattered the Crown, and the private comments of monarchs on the wars and revolutions that defined their reigns. There is a strict, almost priestly etiquette for the processing of such materials. These are not just old papers, they are relics of a living history, and their caretakers are entrusted with the task of protecting the monarchy from its own past.

Diana’s papers constitute one of the most delicate and volatile collections in this environment. Whereas the records of a reigning monarch are bound up with the business of the state, Diana’s legacy is almost wholly personal and emotional. She was the Princess of Hearts, a global symbol whose identity was constructed on a story of fragility, disobedience and intense, protective love for her sons. For decades, royal historians and biographers have speculated about what is in her most private papers, knowing that they contain the potential to change the public’s perception of the most volatile period in contemporary royal history.
