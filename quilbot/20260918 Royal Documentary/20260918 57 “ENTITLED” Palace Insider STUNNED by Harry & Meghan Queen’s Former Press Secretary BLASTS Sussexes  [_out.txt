THE RECOLLECTION OF A PRINCE WHO MISSED

To understand the unbending rigidity of the Crown's position, one only has to look back not four years, but nearly three decades, to the educational disaster of yet another royal couple, who tried to follow the same risky trail. The Queen’s rejection of Harry and Meghan’s plan for a “half-in, half-out” marriage was quick and resolute, sources familiar with the issue said. But it wasn't personal dislike, it was scar tissue from the institution. She knew how the script ended. She had seen it before. At the time, her youngest son, Prince Edward, and his wife, Sophie Rhys-Jones, were the primary protagonists. It is a warning of what happens when the sacred mystique of royalty is contaminated by the profane world of business, and their story is the ghost haunting the ambitions of the Sussexes.

In the 1990s Prince Edward established a television and film production company called Ardent Productions. He was a pretty minor character at the time, and was having difficulty finding a position that was meaningful to him. His ambitions were real; he was the producer of several highly praised historical documentaries, including an entertaining film about the restoration of Windsor Castle. However, his royal rank, which he regarded as his most precious possession, proved to be his sole fatal fault. They resented his access and position, saying he was using his inheritance to skirt the dues they had all paid. The well-established British television networks, a close-knit and fiercely competitive industry, did not regard him as a peer but as a "interloper." So his high quality programs were often relegated to late-night slots, a considerable distance from the prime-time hours, the times in which reputations are made and fortunes are won. The lawyers, the entertainment business execs, wanted one thing from him, and one thing only: content with a royal theme. They didn’t care about his creative vision. They wanted to buy a piece of the brand he represented, and that was never going to happen with the monarchy.

The year 2001 saw the completion of the endeavour in a terrible manner. The media had agreed to respect Prince William's privacy when he began his studies at St. Andrews University. Following a first photo call that was controlled, the press was to leave the situation completely. It was revealed that a video crew from Edward's own company Ardent Productions had been loitering which was against the very deal the palace had negotiated. Edward was the managing director and so totally liable for the issue even when he was not physically there. The incident was a scorching embarrassment that verified the fears of courtiers, and of the Queen herself: the conflict of interest was not hypothetical, but dangerously real.

The alternate job Sophie was pursuing in public relations went even more spectacularly up in flames. She was entrapped in a classic tabloid sting by the News of the World, which has since ceased publication. At that time she was running her own consulting business. An undercover reporter posed as a “fake sheik” and caught her making indiscreet statements about members of the Royal Family, and giving the impression she was trying to exploit her royal connections to recruit consumers. The event had instant and disastrous effects. The Queen was a ruler who valued prudence above anything, and at some time, she'd had enough. She phoned Edward and Sophie, and posed the identical problem that her grandson would face some twenty years later: you have to make a decision. It was not only uncomfortable but also unsustainable to be in the palace with one foot and in the boardroom with the other. Edward and Sophie had both chosen to serve their country. They closed down the commercial operations they had been running and bowed their heads to devote themselves to the life of working royals. Over the years, they have quietly become, in the words of Arbiter, "part of the backbone of the monarchy," taking on unglamorous but necessary work in some of the most tough places in the globe. There is little question that Sophie, now Duchess of Edinburgh, is much admired for her work in raising the profile of victims of sexual violence in crisis situations. Her patronage is a high-profile affair, attracting global attention and much-needed funding. Especially when one of the masters is a thousand-year-old Crown. They learned the hard way that you cannot serve two masters.

AN ARCADIAN HOUSE OF GLASS

The fundamental gripe of the Sussexes against the monarchy from the outset has been the monarchy’s purported exploitation of the media. In the Netflix documentary series they produced and in Harry’s book, Spare, they painted a picture of a cynical institution where rival royal households actively briefed against each other in a cruel game of thrones played out on the front pages of the press. He saw it as a dark art, a betrayal of family, a basic reason for their departure and a major motivation for their journey to California. But this story is a terrible distortion, and a narrative that is dripping with hypocrisy, say a number of people who have run the palace’s media machine. One of the most frequent accusations of Prince Harry is that he has become an adept at the very techniques he so openly rejects.

Dicky Arbiter has been very clear that he doesn’t believe there is a “big agenda” to ruin the Sussexes. Briefing and leaking is a reality in the high stakes world of politics and monarchy, but he says the idea of a prepared war room going after Harry and Meghan is a dream. He cites a model that’s far more incendiary and instructive: the media war that raged between Charles and Diana in the 1990s. Even then, during the height of the royal feud, dubbed the most toxic in recent history, Arbiter maintained that Charles would not initiate a tit-for-tat campaign. But, says Arbiter, Charles's chums were jumped on very, very quickly on his orders if they ever started briefing. And then, every once in a while, you’d have Charles’s friends who would start briefing. He made that perfectly clear to me. You don't speak for me.

But the picture of Charles as a reluctant soldier is at odds with the public narrative, shaped to a large extent by Diana's own masterful media manipulation. Charles famously admitted to adultery in his 1994 interview with Jonathan Dimbleby, but only after his marriage had “irretrievably broken down”. Arbiter stresses that not once in that interview, or at any other time, did Charles “rubbish Diana”. He maintained a gentlemanly, if pained, silence, even as Diana was indirectly cooperating with Andrew Morton on her explosive biography and directly telling the world in her Panorama interview, “There were three of us in this marriage.” Charles knew, as Arbiter points out, that the reality was more complex, with affairs on both sides. But he chose not to weaponise the information. He resolved to absorb the blows because he believed that for a future monarch, to rise above the fray was not only a matter of personal taste but a constitutional obligation.

Understanding this history is vital, and the present stalemate between the palace and Harry makes much more sense in that perspective. Having lived through the scorched-earth tactics that dominated the 1990s, the King has a strong aversion to public mudslinging with his own family. He has no plans to retaliate in the press but to preserve the institutional boundaries at a frigid and official distance. This was demonstrated in the latest letter that Lord Chamberlain wrote to the administration. It was an obvious response to the Sussexes’ proclivity to make public private chats about their personal affairs. They have showed an appetite for monetising and weaponising familial relationships for a global audience, and this desire has been evidenced by everything from Oprah Winfrey to Netflix. ENTITLEMENT, a load to carry, a load that is unbearable

Underneath the strategic tensions that have evolved around roles of the media and the royal family is a deeper, more psychological conflict. From the palace’s point of view, the problem can be traced back to the large and, in their opinion, very unpleasant sense of entitlement that the Sussexes have. The emotional basis of the split is the notion, especially held by Harry, that he is entitled to the privileges of royalty without the responsibilities of responsibility. More specifically, it is a charge that emerges in three key areas: security, finances and the continual need for an apology on a daily basis.

The first and loudest of Harry’s emotional gripes on leaving was that he’d been stripped of his 24/7 police protection, paid for by taxpayers. He thought it was a cruel penalty for wanting to be independent and said it was a heartless way of putting his family in danger. But from the organization’s standpoint the argument was simple and free of emotion. This level of security is not provided to anyone based on their birth, but to top working royals who are carrying out official duties on behalf of the state. When Prince Harry decided to abandon his role as a working royal and move to a different nation, he was immediately taken out of the remit of the committee that provides such protection. The palace said his demand was comparable to that of a former cabinet minister who insisted on a state car and driver for life. This was due to his fundamental misunderstanding of his new status as a private person.

Similarly, the allegation that his father had “cut him off financially” was met with the same amount of bewilderment within the Firm. Various accounts have confirmed that King Charles remained to support his son for a significant period after leaving, helping to make the transition as seamless as possible as he constructed his new life. On top of this was the vast inheritance Harry received from his mother’s estate (Princess Diana) and the fortune bestowed on him by the Queen Mother. From the authorities’ point of view, the tale of a prince abandoned in the cold with no money in his pockets was a fabrication designed to evoke pity from the common people. It ignored the vast personal fortune he already possessed, and the commercial agreements that he and Meghan were busily signing, which were worth millions of dollars.

What the Royal Family finds most confusing, however, is the never-ending demand for an apology. Despite the Oprah interview, the Netflix series and the very personal disclosures in Spare — which included charges of racism, emotional abuse and physical assault — the Sussexes continue to maintain that the palace must apologise to them. From the point of view of Windsor this is a strange inversion of the way things really are. They see a family who have been publicly attacked for years by a member of the family, a monarchy whose reputation has been purposely undermined on a world stage, a King and a Prince of Wales publicly insulted by their son and brother. In their view, the apologies should be coming from Montecito to London, not the other way around. Because they think it’s the right way to go. This stalemate is not about pride, it is a clash of worldviews. The Sussexes exist inside a therapeutic milieu that fosters ‘speaking your truth’ and highlighting personal problems. And the monarchy is an institution that has been there for a thousand years, and it is an institution that lays great importance on accountability, discretion and the preservation of the whole. Until Harry and Meghan can 'eat humble pie and accept they were wrong' as Arbiter puts it, it is difficult to reconcile. Until then he cannot fix their relationship. But a cold war is being fought across an ocean of misunderstanding and the standoff will endure.

The Architects of a New Reign Silent

While one branch of the House of Windsor has been plagued by public strife and departure, the other has been quietly and meticulously establishing the foundations for the future. The Prince and Princess of Wales, William and Catherine, are the figure of hope for the redemption of the monarchy. They are the “silent architects” of the future reign, as one courtier puts it, and they are the personification of the stability, duty and long-term vision that the institution so badly wants to project. Arbiter said Catherine was the “stabilising force” of the monarchy and the crucial “foundation on which Prince William’s future reign will rest”. Their strength is not luck but a calculated and deliberate response to the traumatic experiences William went through as a young boy.

Prince William is thought to be one of the most intelligent and self-aware royals of recent times, exactly because he had a ringside seat to the terrible collapse of his parents’ marriage. He saw at first hand the emotional destruction wrought by public feuds, manipulation of the media and the primacy of personal happiness over the stability of the institution. That encounter was the defining template for his own life, however in the opposite direction. His parents have made every mistake in the book and he has learned from all of them. His primary goal has been to make sure his family never has to go through that type of disaster. He has built a strong and secure domestic fortress, with his wife and children at the top of his priorities, that serves as a shield against the strains of public life. When Catherine underwent major abdominal surgery and later revealed she had been diagnosed with cancer, William immediately cleared his notes. He put his family first, not the “duty first, always” attitude common in previous generations.

This stability is mostly owing to the influence of the Middleton family, which is an important part. William found a second family with the Middletons, considering that he had lost his own mother at the age of fifteen. King Charles and Queen Camilla are raising their children, George, Charlotte and Louis, with a careful balance of royal know-how and normal childhood experiences. They are gently introducing their children to public life in a way that avoids the “goldfish bowl” anguish that both William and Harry suffered. This carefully constructed family unit is not only William's personal sanctuary, but is also the single most important strategic asset held by the monarchy.

THE HOLLOW THRONE AND THE UNINTENTIONAL ABDICATION In the United States

For years the popular narrative has been that King Charles has harboured a secret ambition to create a “slimmed-down monarchy”. The idea was that he would trim the fat, cutting loose the peripheral dukes and cousins to create a more streamlined, more modern and cost-effective institution focused on the direct line of succession. But Dicky Arbiter says it a media invention. “Charles has never said anything about a smaller monarchy,” he says. The truth is far more dramatic and less calculated than fiction. The monarchy is shrinking, but not by design. It is shrinking by default, because of death, scandal and abdication.

Now, in recent years, there has been a terrible purging of the royal echelons. After their deaths Queen Elizabeth II and Prince Philip could not remain the two most powerful people in the institution. The scandal of Prince Andrew’s association with Jeffrey Epstein saw him essentially ostracised from the Firm, and pushed him into retirement from public life. And then there was of course the departure of Harry and Meghan, which came as an extension of their self-imposed exile, which robbed the monarchy of two of its most charismatic and widely recognisable figures. In just a few short years, five of the most prominent members of the working Royal Family died.

The timing is also a part of the problem. The new generation of royalists are old people. By contrast the Duke of Kent is in his 90s and the Duke of Gloucester and his wife are both in their 80s. The king and queen, Charles and Camilla, are both in their late 70s. Most of these dedicated characters will be either retired or dead by the time Prince William is crowned king. The simple principles of biology and the passage of time will do the work of shrinking the size of the monarchy he inherits. The working core will most likely be himself, Queen Catherine and possibly even their offspring if they elect to engage in formal capacities.

It presents the Crown with a serious long-term strategic issue. The Royal Family currently undertakes hundreds of official engagements throughout the United Kingdom and the Commonwealth, and supports thousands of charity and other groups financially. This large network of patronage allows the monarchy to maintain its connection to the ordinary public and its soft power. Who will do this labour in ages to come? The departure of Harry and Meghan was not only a family drama, it was a devastating loss of personnel. The younger generation was meant to be the ones who would push the institution forward and be a vital part of William’s support system. Harry’s decision to depart has put a load on his brother’s shoulders that is perilously near to unbearable. He will inherit a crown, but he may well find himself at the helm of an emaciated organization, its reach and relevance diminished simply because there are not enough hands to complete the duty. In a sad twist of irony, Prince Harry may have thought he was striking a blow for freedom, but he may instead have succeeded in undermining the same institution that his brother is destined to head, leaving him to rule over a smaller and more fragile kingdom. What a horrible irony this is.

The United Kingdom’s monarchy will be there. It has known hard times before, including abdication, war, scandal and tragedy. Its greatest strength is the ability to adapt and keep going under changing situations. But it will come out of this turbulent time battered. “The falling out with the Duke and Duchess of Sussex has created deep and lasting wounds. These wounds do not only harm the family. They affect the fabric of the institution itself. This has brought to light a generational gap, a clash of cultures, and a fundamental argument over the meaning of service in the 21st century. King Charles’s reign will be forever defined by his struggle to manage this crisis within the royal family, and Prince William’s fate will be decided by the fallout from this internal crisis. The Crown still stands but the family is shattered and the road ahead is a lonely journey. It is haunted by the spirits of those who chose to choose another life, leaving the few left alive to carry the full weight of a legacy a thousand years in the making.
