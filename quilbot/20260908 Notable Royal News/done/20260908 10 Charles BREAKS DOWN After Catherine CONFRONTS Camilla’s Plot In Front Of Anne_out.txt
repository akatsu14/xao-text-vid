THE GATHERING STORM IN THE KING'S QUARTERS

This day, however, it was the chamber where the sense of reality, and the underpinnings of his authority, would be ruthlessly undermined.

Charles, the frail peacemaker, the guy whose whole public life had been a pursuit for harmony and a frantic avoidance of confrontation, believed it was a simple misunderstanding. THE KING'S FALL

When Catherine had finished speaking the last words lingered in the air like the smoke after a shot. All the proof was there. The unseen hand was revealed. In a normal royal assembly this was when the monarch takes charge, gives a sharp command and brings order back. But that was not to be. But an unsaid signal passed across the room. Princess Anne stood up carefully and brushed the front of her jacket. Catherine stood straight, posture perfect, eyes on the King. Camilla hesitated a while, then pulled back her chair and stood. The official business of the meeting was ended. The jury was still out.

But Charles stayed sat, stiff and strangely still in his high-backed chair. He looked down at the papers without seeing them. Slowly he brought his hands to his face, massaging his forehead with shaking, old fingers. At last he spoke, but not in the voice of a king.
