A SCENE READY TO surrender

The choice of place was a stroke of psychological staging. To meet in Buckingham Palace would have made the meeting an affair of state formality, of the institution and its officers. A meeting at Clarence House, the King's longtime London home, would have been a negotiation on his territory, giving him a tacit, paternalistic edge. The heart of the dynasty is Windsor. It is where royal weddings and private Christmases take place, the fortress that protected the family during the Blitz, the last, sacred burial place of generations of sovereigns, including the late Queen Elizabeth II herself.

The quiet, powerful instruction was to bring in the important players to Windsor. It was an order to leave the armor of state at the door. Come not as Prince of Wales, but as a son. The guards are to be let down in a chamber prepared with care to give the effect of a family drawing-room. Defenses are to be broken down. The whole tale of the Windsor Summit, the plot and the unraveling, is there in the harsh text of its guest list. For the outsider, a list of names is an administrative issue. It’s the whole chessboard for a veteran player in the merciless game of royal politics. The discussion was presented to the Prince of Wales as a conversation among the most senior members of the family, with only a handful of the most trusted, long-serving household aides attending. From William’s standpoint this was constitutionally and personally impossible.
