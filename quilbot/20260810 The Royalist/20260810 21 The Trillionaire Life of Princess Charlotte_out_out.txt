The First Daughter of the Perth Agreement A FIRST DAUGHTER OF THE PERTH AGREEMENT

Princess Charlotte’s most prized possession is not a diamond tiara or a centuries-old palace, but her irreplaceable place in the royal family’s line of succession – something that is historically significant and perhaps the most valuable thing she has. She is the first woman in the direct line to the British monarchy whose place is guarantyd by the constitution and not subject to the arcane laws of male-preference primogeniture which were discriminatory in origin. This revolutionary status was a direct result of the Perth Agreement of 2011, a major piece of legislation agreed upon by the leaders of all sixteen Commonwealth nations. This law radically and forever modified the rules of royal succession. The legislation was formally enacted in 2015, a few months before the birth of her younger brother Prince Louis, and it means that the line of succession to the throne depends only on the order of birth, not on the sex of the child. This is a big constitutional advance in the view of Princess Charlotte. It also confirms her status as third in line to the throne, a position that is now beyond doubt and challenge, behind her father, Prince William, and her elder brother, Prince George.

It is enough to study the life and career of her own great-aunt, Princess Anne, to gain a full grasp of the revolutionary nature of this legal change. Anne is Queen Elizabeth II's second child and was born in 1950. When she was born, she was the second in line to the throne. However, she was later bumped down the line of succession due to the births of her two younger brothers, Andrew and Edward. She was constitutionally disadvantaged for ever only because she was a woman. This was regardless of the fact that she had worked relentlessly all her life and had built a reputation as one of the most respected and conscientious members of the royal family. Constitutional academics and historians agree that the Perth Agreement righted a historical injustice that had been entrenched for generations. This accord brought the old-fashioned rules of the monarchy into line with modern ideas of equality and merit. Princess Charlotte is the first royal baby and the first living, breathing symbol of this new, more equitable era. She will never be replaced by a younger sibling and that gives her a level of constitutional stability, dignity and life-long purpose never before seen in the history of the British Crown for a female “spare”.

Her life will be the first major test case for this new model of succession, one that is more equal, as to whether or not it will create a better functioning, and ultimately more long-lasting, royal dynasty for the stormy ages to come.
