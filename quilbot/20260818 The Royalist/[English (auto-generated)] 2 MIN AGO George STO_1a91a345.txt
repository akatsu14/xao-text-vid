The conflict was not one of land or titles, but of something far more basic: the raising of a future king.

The first skirmish came at Windsor during a smaller, private family meal. In advance of the senior royals’ arrival, household servants had laid out informal seating plans for a series of planned state receptions and summer parties. As Catherine went through the files, a subtle but extremely disturbing pattern began to emerge. In practically every plan for every coming event, Prince George had been purposely and carefully separated from her. Older courtiers hemmed him in, senior dukes fenced him off, and he was stranded among distant, ancient relations. It was a slow, systematic disengagement.

Catherine discussed the problem with the home planners to clarify the new policy – and Queen Camilla herself got into the conversation. She did not dispute her part in it, she admitted it. THE ACCOUNT OF THE KING

Back at the meal, the atmosphere was thick enough to cut with a knife. The ceremonial lunch remained absolutely frozen, a scene of royal paralysis. Prince George stood up straight and firm alongside the chair behind Catherine, his hand resting firmly on its polished wood back. He refused to sit in his new seat and as the future heir to the kingdom stood, royal tradition insisted no one else in the room could sit either. A schoolboy's subtle rebellion had held the entire machinery of the court to ransom.

Camilla, whose authority was now being openly challenged, tried to rescue the situation. She came toward George, her voice no longer that of the benign matriarch, but the severe disciplinarian. She said royal sitting was dictated by status and duty, not personal choice. She said, with his increasing public obligations in the future, he would have to learn to seat where protocol put him, no matter who sat next to him. It was a typical palace guilt trip, a way of making an eleven-year-old feel he was failing in his sacred duties.

But George did not waver. He glanced up at the Queen and posed a simple, devastatingly logical question which took away all her pretense. Why had his seat been assigned to his mother on the official seating schedule approved that very morning, only to be reassigned moments before the lunch began? He was fighting a sly last-minute inversion of royal protocol.

It was Catherine’s signal. Her voice was low and controlled as she spoke but had a strong warning register. She was courteous but angry. THE NEW COURT LAW 

The ceremonial lunch continued finally, but it was an agony of strained endurance. Each clink of a silver fork on beautiful porcelain was a thunderclap in the stiflingly quiet chamber. King Charles tried to sew the afternoon back together with polite, superficial chit-chat about the weather and charity schedules. William's answers were short, businesslike, and chillingly stiff. One witness there said Camilla hardly touched her food. She stared straight ahead, the weight of her ultimate political defeat on her. Beside his mother, Prince George kept silent, eating his lunch with impeccable etiquette, fully knowing that he just fought his first significant battle within the royal gates, and won.

After the last dessert plates were cleared, the royal party broke up with unparalleled swiftness. No lingering coffees, no casual family get-togethers. Everyone retreated en masse to their individual suites to digest the damage. Alone Camilla went to her sitting-room, the big oak doors closing securely behind her. It was said that she was shook to the core, not merely by George’s rebellion but by how quickly and totally Charles had united with William and Catherine against her. She had her authority crushed openly and firmly in an attempt to establish hers as matriarch of the Firm.

Meanwhile William and Catherine were sitting with their kid in their own apartments. Catherine wrapped her arms around him, thanking him for his courage. But she also gave him a piece of vital mother-wisdom. She informed him that she would always stand by him, but that it was never his role to fight adult political conflicts or to maintain her place in the family. She wanted him to be a child, free from the poison of royal power struggles. His parents, it is told, were shocked by George’s answer. He informed his mother he was not trying to defend her status, just his own right to select where he felt safe. The youngster already had an extraordinary, innate knowledge of personal space, something that many adult royals take a lifetime to master, and some never do.

Later that afternoon Prince William was invited for a strictly private meeting with King Charles. No aides, no advisors. Just a dad and a kid. Looking very tired the King asked William if he and Catherine really thought he and Camilla were trying to keep George away from his mother. William didn’t give a courteous or sugar-coated answer. “It’s hard to see it as anything other than a deliberate strategy when last minute, clandestine changes are made to isolate an eleven year old boy,” he informed his father. Then he gave a strong warning: if they tried to meddle with how they were raising their children anymore, they would be totally withdrawn from any non-essential family occasions. That was clearly the message. The Waleses were not messing around.

Charles understood the threat he was facing. He could not afford to lose William, Catherine and George. For they are the future of the monarchy, its link with the people, its life. He knew what he had to do when faced with the choice of backing his wife’s excessive expectations of etiquette or protecting his relationship with his heir. He told William that the line would be established in stone right away, a permanent, official boundary.

There are repercussions that extend far beyond the mere fact that the luncheon was canceled. The power structure of the royal household was permanently altered as a result of this. In less than forty-eight hours, King Charles had given a fresh and strict internal edict to all of the employees. The newly implemented regulation is straightforward: the responsibility for Prince George, Princess Charlotte, and Prince Louis is solely the responsibility of the Prince and Princess of Wales. Without the full consent of William and Catherine, no senior royal, not even Queen Camilla, is allowed to make any changes to their schedule, seating arrangements, or public exposure. State etiquette would continue to control the most important public appearances; nevertheless, at any private or family-oriented gathering, the emotional demands and parental decisions of the Wales family would, from this point forward, take precedence over any arbitrary judicial convention.

After some time, the tale became public. The answer from the general public was unequivocal and decisive. They were not witnesses to a child that was pampered. They observed a small boy who stood up for his mother and had an incredible amount of loyalty. The picture of a future king sitting down next to Catherine in such a calm and cool manner reverberated across the public, enhancing the public's admiration for her and drawing attention to the high moral standards that her children have raised. Camilla learned a humble lesson that will stay with her for the rest of her life. Fear and cold, old-fashioned manipulation are not effective when it comes to modern monarchy politics.

And when the next great family gathering took place, the name card of Prince George was firmly, securely, and indelibly placed next to Catherine's. Nobody actually attempted to move it. While George was there, he was able to see his seat for himself. After giving his mother a slight smile in a low voice, he brought out a chair and sat down in the spot that was rightfully his. It was not enough for Prince George to simply save his seat at a luncheon; he did much more than that. He held his own with a simple place card. A defense was made for his childhood. It was his mother that he upheld. In addition, he conveyed to the entire court the kind of king he will one day become: a king who is aware that genuine duty must never be permitted to be allowed to kill mankind.
