THE THEATER OF BIRTH: SUSPICION, SECRECY, AND THE RIDDLE OF ARCHIE

The birth of a royal baby has long been a moment of national celebration and constitutional significance. It is a moment of continuity, a sign that the monarchy endures. But the birth of Archie Harrison Mountbatten-Windsor in May 2019 was wrapped in so much secrecy, misdirection and theater that it bucked centuries of convention and triggered a firestorm of public skepticism. The Sussexes wanted to control every detail of the event, ignoring the traditions that govern a royal birth. They would not say when the Duchess had been taken in labor. They declined the usual photo call on the hospital steps, a moment of public communion that Princess Diana and the Duchess of Cambridge had graciously offered. For a while they would not even say which hospital the birth had occurred in.

But this preoccupation with privacy created a vacuum of knowledge that was rapidly filled with negative supposition. There were contradictions within the official account itself. Buckingham Palace's original statement said the Duchess was in labor - but journalists quickly uncovered that the baby had been born hours earlier. Many perceived the willful misrepresentation as a fundamental contempt for the people and the press. Why do we need such games? What were they trying to conceal? The questions grew louder at the tightly-controlled picture call two days later at Windsor Castle. The blanket was wrapped over the baby so tightly that only a small part of his face showed. Harry, looking agitated, physically turned the baby away from the cameras, allowing only one reporter to ask questions. It all seemed so contrived, so unnatural, so utterly freaky.

This bizarre behavior gave rise to a number of conspiracy theories, the most infamous being the “moon bump” hypothesis which claimed that the Duchess had not carried the child herself. The mainstream may have pooh-poohed it, but the hypothesis took hold because of the couple’s behavior. Online, pictures of the Duchess bending down in high heels with a flexibility that many moms found physically impossible in their third trimester were constantly examined. Their unwillingness to be open about it, to share their happiness in the normal way, had produced a climate in which the wildest hypotheses did not seem so implausible after all. They had taken a moment of national togetherness and turned it into a time of conflict and doubt. Royal historians point out that although the monarch’s job is mostly symbolic in the 21st century, the relationship with the public remains its lifeblood. The Sussexes were cutting that all-important tie by walling themselves off, by treating the birth of the seventh in line to the throne as a private, Hollywood-style ceremony. Again, this was their way of saying that they meant to function as an entirely independent organization, a royal power base that was answerable to nobody but themselves.

It was meant to harm the image of the monarchy on the world stage, particularly in the Commonwealth. They are becoming “box office poison” in Hollywood’s merciless calculus, their only proven money-maker the trashing of the royals, and they have exhausted that well. The monarchy has survived this ten-year attack, but not undamaged. The Sussexes have done terrible damage in their pursuit of personal vindication and financial gain.
