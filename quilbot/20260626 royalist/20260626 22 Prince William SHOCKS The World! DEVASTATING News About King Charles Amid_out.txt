A hundred years of full pressure.

This slowness is a feature, not a bug. It insures not just continuation. It also precludes the formation of a competing court . It projects an aura of unbreakable stability . OPERATION SAFEGUARD: PLANS FOR THE WORLD END

But behind closed doors at Buckingham Palace and Windsor Castle is a much more methodical and terrifying procedure. The monarchy has persisted for more than a thousand years not by accident but by meticulous and foresighted preparation for it. For thousands of years courtiers and public workers have invented elaborate protocols for every conceivable event from royal births and weddings to state funerals and not least successions. The protocols go back generations. These preparations, frequently known by codenames, such as the well-known "Operation London Bridge" for the execution of the late Queen, are made long before they are ever needed. But in the past few weeks there have been stories suggesting some of these long-standing contingency plans are being re-examined with a fresh sense of urgency that is raising worry.

The quarrels within the palace walls are no longer limited to the government of the day. They are even more intensely throwing their energies into the defense of the future. Advisers are reported to be looking at the constitutional structure put in place to protect the continuity of the crown and looking at contingency plans which have been largely inactive for years. This is not because one expects a speedy change, but because it is a necessary aspect of the concept of the monarchy that its stability can never be left to chance. “Get ready for all the possibilities long before they actually happen. The “institutional mindset” has been key to its survival through wars, revolutions, scandals over the decades.

But this time is different from past moments somehow. These are not only paper exercises in the efforts being examined. They are being researched in such a specialized way that it is possible that they will be needed. In terms of advice the “what if” is no longer asked in a generic sense, but rather in a more precise sense. What alterations are to be made in respect of the likely results? How critical would it be to have a communications plan in place? What constitutional processes would be involved in a brief lapse versus a more permanent lapse? These are questions of a specific and pragmatic nature and the fact that they are being asked with such a high level of care says all there is to say about the level of respect that is being given to the issue.

This planning aims to cover a wide range of activities. Assistants don’t concentrate on a single path forward, but instead consider a lot of different alternative outcomes at once. Some of the proposals suggest a temporary change to the King's duties, for example giving the Counsellors of State a larger administrative role. Some argue for more serious and constitutionally problematic outcomes. “We are thinking, planning and rehearsing for all the possible outcomes with the same overriding objective, whatever happens to the King, the institution has to remain stable and the transfer of power when it happens has to be seamless.

They are preparing inside for what is likely to happen but that they are not ready to embrace yet. “They are having conversations they can’t confirm, they are looking at plans they can’t discuss,” he claimed. The alternative is to be honest about planning for contingencies and this would put you in the same boat. The signal, however, is clear: the situation has passed the level of uncertainty that can be controlled, and it is time to think about the future in a serious and active way.
